Why Families Are Moving
to Ras Al Khaimah

Dubai Off Plan Homes  ·  10 November 2026  ·  9 min read
Home› Blog› Families Moving to Ras Al Khaimah

Families are moving to Ras Al Khaimah for space, value and a slower pace of life, without leaving the UAE. A villa with a garden, a beach close by, mountains on the doorstep and school fees that leave room in the budget: that is the trade more Dubai families are weighing up as rents and fees in the city keep climbing.

This guide looks at what is actually driving the move, what everyday life costs, how the schools compare, which communities families are choosing, and what to check before you commit.

What Is Driving the Move to Ras Al Khaimah?

For most families, it comes down to three things. The first is space: the budget that rents a two-bedroom apartment in a busy part of Dubai can rent a villa with a garden in Ras Al Khaimah. The second is cost, with housing and school fees typically well below Dubai levels. The third is lifestyle: beaches, mountains, mangroves and quieter streets, in an emirate where everyday life feels less rushed.

Flexible and hybrid working has made the move far more practical. A parent who goes into a Dubai office two or three days a week can now realistically live an hour up the coast, and many businesses are setting up in the emirate itself.

More garden, more beach and more left over at the end of the month, while staying in the UAE.

The Bigger Picture: An Emirate on the Rise

Ras Al Khaimah is no longer a quiet alternative. The emirate recorded a record 1.35 million overnight visitors in 2025, followed by more than 670,000 visitors in the first half of 2026, its strongest first half on record. The US$5.1 billion Wynn Al Marjan Island resort is expected to open in September 2027, and Ras Al Khaimah is targeting 3.5 million visitors a year by 2030.

That growth is bringing jobs, restaurants, healthcare and transport with it. Residential property sales reached AED 12.4 billion across around 6,600 transactions in 2025, and around 8,400 new homes are planned by 2028. A new private aviation terminal at Ras Al Khaimah International Airport is also scheduled for early 2027.

For families who buy rather than rent, that momentum matters. Ras Al Khaimah freehold areas are open to international buyers, and owning means you are not exposed to rising rents as the emirate becomes more popular.

Cost of Living for Families

Housing is where the difference shows most. Expat guides put rents in Ras Al Khaimah roughly 30% to 45% below Dubai, and overall living costs including rent around 25% to 35% lower. Local cost guides estimate a family of four can live comfortably on around AED 18,000 to 25,000 a month in Ras Al Khaimah, against AED 35,000 or more for a similar lifestyle in Dubai Marina.

Not everything is cheaper. Fuel prices are set nationally, so petrol costs the same as in Dubai, and if one parent commutes south several times a week, fuel and time need to be part of the sum.

Schools in Ras Al Khaimah

School choice used to be the main hesitation for families. That is changing quickly. Ras Al Khaimah has 34 private schools, all regulated and inspected by the Ras Al Khaimah Department of Knowledge (RAK DOK), covering British, American, IB, Indian and UAE Ministry curricula.

In the 2026 inspections, the emirate recorded its first ever Outstanding school, with five schools moving up from Acceptable to Good and two from Good to Very Good. For the 2026 to 2027 academic year, three new private schools were approved, adding around 3,000 places, and RAK Academy opened a new secondary school at its Al Hamra campus in August 2026.

Fees are a major part of the appeal. RAK Academy, one of the best-known British and IB schools, charges roughly AED 17,000 to 42,000 a year depending on the year group, well below many Dubai equivalents. Popular schools do have waiting lists, so apply early.

Beaches, Mountains and Space to Breathe

Ras Al Khaimah is the UAE's outdoor emirate. Jebel Jais, the country's highest peak, offers hiking, cooler air and the world-famous zipline. The coast is lined with beaches and resorts, the mangroves are perfect for kayaking, and golf courses at Al Hamra and Tower Links add more to do at weekends. For children, that means more time outside and fewer hours in the car to get there.

Where Families Are Choosing to Live

Al Hamra Village

What it is: an established coastal community with villas, townhouses and apartments around a golf course, beach and marina.

Amenities: Al Hamra Mall, the golf club, beach access and RAK Academy's Al Hamra campus, now including a secondary school.

Who it suits: families who want a settled, ready-made community with schools close by.

Mina Al Arab

What it is: a waterfront community of villas and apartments set around lagoons, beaches and protected mangroves.

Amenities: Beaches, walking routes, cafés and nature reserves, with new launches continuing to add homes.

Who it suits: families who want a calmer, nature-led setting near the water.

Al Marjan Island

What it is: a set of man-made islands that is becoming the emirate's resort and hospitality hub, home to the Wynn development.

Amenities: Hotels, beach clubs, restaurants and a growing number of branded residences.

Who it suits: families who also want strong holiday-let potential, or who love a resort atmosphere.

Al Mairid

What it is: a coastal district on the northern side of Ras Al Khaimah city, around 10 minutes from downtown, now home to the new Mira Coral Bay masterplan.

Amenities: Beach, a planned yacht club, beach clubs and resorts within Mira Coral Bay, close to city schools and services.

Who it suits: families who want new, fully furnished waterfront living close to the city. Read our Mira Coral Bay guide.

Family Moving Checklist

  • ◆ Map the commute for whoever works in Dubai, at peak times, before choosing an area
  • ◆ Shortlist schools first and check waiting lists, then look at homes nearby
  • ◆ Compare renting with buying, especially as rents in popular communities rise
  • ◆ Check the community's handover date if you are buying off-plan
  • ◆ Look at healthcare and nurseries close to your shortlisted areas
  • ◆ Visit at the weekend and on a weekday to get a feel for both

Common Mistakes, and What to Do Instead

Choosing a home before a school

Instead: Secure school places first. The best-rated schools fill early, and the right school should shape where you live.

Underestimating the commute

Instead: Test the drive at rush hour, and agree hybrid working before you move if one parent stays in a Dubai job.

Assuming every community is family-ready

Instead: Some areas are built around holiday homes and hotels. Check for parks, nurseries and year-round residents.

Waiting until prices catch up

Instead: Values in key communities rose by double digits over the past year. If you plan to stay long term, compare the cost of buying now against years of rising rent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are families moving to Ras Al Khaimah?+
Mainly for space and value: larger homes with gardens, lower rents and school fees than Dubai, beaches and mountains close by, and a quieter pace of life, while staying within an hour or so of Dubai.
Is Ras Al Khaimah cheaper than Dubai?+
Generally yes. Expat guides put rents around 30% to 45% lower and overall living costs including rent around 25% to 35% lower, though fuel costs the same across the UAE.
Are there good schools in Ras Al Khaimah?+
Yes, and the sector is improving fast. There are 34 private schools inspected by RAK DOK, the first Outstanding rating was awarded in 2026, and around 3,000 new places were added for 2026 to 2027.
How far is Ras Al Khaimah from Dubai?+
Around an hour by car for most journeys, depending on where you live in Ras Al Khaimah and where you work in Dubai.
Which areas of Ras Al Khaimah are best for families?+
Al Hamra Village and Mina Al Arab are the most established family choices, while Al Marjan Island and Al Mairid offer newer waterfront options.
Can expats buy property in Ras Al Khaimah?+
Yes. International buyers can own freehold property in Ras Al Khaimah's designated freehold areas, including Al Hamra, Mina Al Arab, Al Marjan Island and Al Mairid.
When does the Wynn resort open?+
Wynn Al Marjan Island is expected to open in September 2027.
Is it better to rent or buy in Ras Al Khaimah?+
It depends on how long you plan to stay. With rents rising in popular communities, buying, especially off-plan with a staged payment plan, can make sense for families planning to stay several years.
