Dubai Off-Plan Payment Plans: How They Work in 2026

Dubai Off Plan Homes  ·  Updated September 2026  ·  10 min read
Home› Blog› Dubai Off-Plan Payment Plans

Pay as it is built

Dubai off-plan payment plans let you pay for a property in stages, usually 10% to 20% upfront, instalments during construction and a balance on handover, with some plans continuing after you get the keys. Developer plans are normally interest-free, and your payments are held in a regulated escrow account.

Dubai off-plan payment plans are the main reason so many international buyers choose new-build property in the city. Instead of needing the full price or a mortgage on day one, you spread the cost over the construction period, and in many cases beyond it, directly with the developer and without paying interest.

The catch is that plans which look similar on paper can be very different in practice. This guide explains every common structure, shows the real numbers on a worked example, and covers the costs and pitfalls that headline ratios leave out. What Is an Off-Plan Payment Plan?

An off-plan payment plan is the instalment schedule set out in your Sale and Purchase Agreement (SPA). It tells you how much you pay, and when, from the day you reserve the unit until the property is fully paid for. Most plans have four parts:

The four parts of a payment plan
Down paymentUsually 10% to 20%, paid when you sign
Construction instalmentsSpread across the build, often 2 to 4 years
Handover paymentThe balance due when you receive the keys
Post-handover instalmentsOptional, paid after handover over 1 to 5 years

The headline ratio, such as 60/40, normally describes how much is paid before handover versus on or after it. Instalments can be construction-linked, meaning each payment falls due when the building reaches a verified milestone, or time-linked, meaning payments fall on fixed calendar dates whatever the progress on site. Construction-linked plans give you more protection if a project runs late.

Why Payment Plans Make Dubai Different

In most property markets, buying a new-build still means a large deposit and a bank mortgage. In Dubai, the developer effectively finances the purchase during construction, interest-free. That is a major reason why off-plan sales make up a large share of all transactions in the city, and why buyers from the UK and Europe often find they can enter the market with far less upfront capital than they expected.

This is backed by strong buyer protection. By law, off-plan payments go into a RERA-regulated escrow account for that specific project, and the developer can only draw on it as construction progress is verified. Your purchase is also registered with the Dubai Land Department through the Oqood system, giving you a recorded interest in the unit long before the title deed is issued.

The market has also become more competitive in how plans are structured. Post-handover periods have lengthened through 2026, with three to five year tails now common in some communities. For buyers, that means more choice, but also more need to read the detail.

"The headline ratio tells you the split. The timing, the fees and the resale terms tell you what the plan is really worth."

The Main Types of Payment Plan

Construction heavy

80/20 and 70/30 plans

You pay most of the price during construction and a smaller balance of 20% or 30% on handover. These plans are common with larger, established developers. They suit cash buyers who are comfortable paying as the building rises and want a small final bill, or buyers who plan to take a mortgage for the handover balance.

On our site: Ellington Cove and Meriva Collection on Dubai Islands both use a 70/30 structure.

The market standard

60/40 and 50/50 plans

A balanced split between construction and handover. With a 60/40 plan, a typical pattern is 20% at booking, 40% in construction instalments and 40% on completion. A 50/50 plan halves the construction commitment again. These suit investors who want to keep more cash free during the build and either pay or refinance the balance at handover.

On our site: 113 Residences, Hado Beyond, Soma Residences and Mira Coral Bay (Ras Al Khaimah) are on 50/50 plans; Wilds Residences uses 65/35.

Handover heavy

40/60 and 30/70 plans

Less is paid during construction and more on completion. These are attractive if you expect your cash position to improve before handover, or if you want to limit your exposure while the project is being built. The trade-off is a large payment on completion, so you need a clear plan for funding it.

On our site: Arancia Yards and Hudayriyat Golf Estates (Abu Dhabi) use 40/60; Linar (Sharjah) uses 30/70.

Pay after the keys

Post-handover plans

Part of the price, often 20% to 60%, is paid after you receive the keys, typically across one to five years. Post-handover tails have been getting longer in 2026, with three to five years now common. These plans suit investors who want the rent to help pay off the property, and end users who want to move in before they have paid in full.

On our site: RAW District 2 offers a post-handover 60/40 option.

Small, steady amounts

1% monthly plans

After a down payment of around 10%, you pay 1% of the price every month, often continuing for a period after handover. This gives the most predictable cash flow of any structure and suits buyers who prefer to budget from monthly income rather than lump sums. The total term is long, so check exactly how many monthly payments fall before and after handover.

Worked Example on AED 1.5 Million

Here is how a standard 60/40 plan looks on an AED 1.5 million apartment with a 30-month build, using 20% at booking, 40% spread evenly across construction and 40% on handover.

PaymentWhenAmount (AED)
Down payment (20%)On signing300,000
DLD registration fee (4%)On signing60,000
Oqood and admin feesOn signingA few thousand
Construction instalments (40%)Over 30 months600,000 (about 20,000 a month)
Handover payment (40%)On completion600,000

So you would need around AED 365,000 at the start, not the AED 300,000 the headline down payment suggests, because the 4% DLD fee is calculated on the full price and is due upfront. On a 1% monthly plan at the same price, the monthly commitment would be about AED 15,000 after a smaller down payment. Try your own figures in the payment plan calculator.

Real developer schedules are usually tied to construction milestones rather than equal monthly amounts, so your actual instalments will be lumpier than this. The totals are what matter for budgeting.

Buyer Checklist

  • Ask for the full payment schedule with dates or milestones, not just the headline ratio
  • Confirm whether instalments are construction-linked or time-linked
  • Budget for the 4% DLD fee on the full price at signing, plus Oqood and admin fees
  • Check the project's escrow account details and make every payment into it
  • Confirm your SPA will be registered on Oqood in your name
  • Read the resale clause: the percentage you must pay before you can sell, and any transfer fee
  • For post-handover plans, check how many years and whether the balance is secured against the property
  • Plan now for how you will fund the handover payment, whether cash, mortgage or rental income
  • Allow for service charges from handover in your running costs

Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

Mistake: Comparing plans by headline ratio alone.

Instead: A 40/60 plan with the 60% due at handover is very different from a 40/60 spread over five years after handover. Always compare the full schedule.

Mistake: Forgetting the 4% DLD fee is due upfront on the full price.

Instead: Add it to your down payment when working out how much cash you need on day one.

Mistake: Assuming you can sell at any time.

Instead: Many developers only allow resale after 30% to 40% has been paid. Check this if you might want to exit before handover.

Mistake: Leaving the handover payment to chance.

Instead: If you plan to use a mortgage, speak to a bank well before completion. Lending criteria and valuations can differ from the price you agreed years earlier.

Mistake: Paying anywhere other than the project escrow account.

Instead: Only pay into the escrow account named in your SPA. This is your protection if anything goes wrong.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common off-plan payment plan in Dubai?

Construction-linked plans such as 60/40, 70/30 and 50/50 are the most common, where the first figure is paid during construction and the second on handover. Post-handover plans and 1% monthly plans have become increasingly popular in 2026.

How much deposit do I need for off-plan property in Dubai?

Most developers ask for a down payment of 10% to 20% of the price when you sign, plus the 4% Dubai Land Department fee, which is calculated on the full price. Some high-demand launches also ask for a fixed booking amount at the reservation stage.

Do I pay interest on a developer payment plan?

No. Developer payment plans in Dubai are normally interest-free. You pay the agreed price in instalments, which is one of the main advantages over a mortgage. Check the sale agreement for any admin charges or late payment penalties.

What is a post-handover payment plan?

A plan where part of the price is paid after you receive the keys, typically over one to five years. It lets you move in or start renting before the property is fully paid for, so rental income can help cover the remaining instalments.

Is my money safe while the property is being built?

Off-plan payments in Dubai must go into a RERA-regulated escrow account for the project, and the developer can only draw funds as construction milestones are verified. Your purchase should also be registered with the Dubai Land Department through Oqood.

Can I sell an off-plan property before I finish paying?

Often yes, but many developers only allow resale once a set percentage of the price has been paid, commonly 30% to 40%, and may charge a transfer fee. Check the resale clause in your sale agreement before you buy.

What happens if I miss a payment?

Developers can apply late fees, and after a formal notice process through the Dubai Land Department they may be able to cancel the contract and retain part of what you have paid, depending on how far construction has progressed. If you expect a problem, speak to the developer early about restructuring.

This guide is general information about how off-plan payment plans typically work in Dubai and is not financial or legal advice. Payment plans, fees and resale terms vary by developer and project, so always check the details in your Sale and Purchase Agreement.
